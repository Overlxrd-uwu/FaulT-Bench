==================================================
Scenario: kath5-r4-virtual-link-removed
Topology: Kath5
Fault family: OSPF virtual-link removed
==================================================

[PROMPT-TO-AGENT]
After a router reload on r4, OSPF routes for the 18.x.x.x
loopbacks (r8's loopback range) and 80.0.0.0/8 (Area 2)
disappeared from r1's routing table. r1 still has routes
for Area 0 and Area 1. r4 reports Area 1 is healthy.
Investigate.

[INJECTION]
device: r4
action: remove the area 1 virtual-link to r8
cmd: |
  vtysh -c "configure terminal" \
        -c "router ospf" \
        -c "no area 1 virtual-link 18.1.4.1" \
        -c "exit" -c "exit"

[POST-INJECT-CHECK]
r4   vtysh -c "show ip ospf interface vlink0" 2>&1 | grep -c "Interface"  => expect 0
r1   vtysh -c "show ip route" | grep -c "80\.\|18\."                       => expect 0

[GROUND-TRUTH]
Affected component: r4 OSPF virtual-link to r8 (router-id 18.1.4.1)
Mechanism: r8 is the ABR for Area 2 (which contains the 80.0.0.0/8
  network and r8's 18.x loopbacks). Area 2 is not directly attached
  to Area 0 (the backbone) — it touches Area 1 via r4 and Area 1
  to Area 0 via r3/r5. OSPF requires every non-backbone area to
  have either a direct connection to Area 0 or a virtual-link
  through a transit area. r4 provides that virtual-link for Area 2
  via Area 1 (transit) to r8 (router-id 18.1.4.1). With the
  virtual-link removed, r8 cannot inject Area 2 LSAs into the
  backbone, and the rest of the network loses all routes for
  Area 2 prefixes.
Localization: r4 (and r8, which is the other endpoint, but the
  primary configuration change is on r4)
Root cause: Area 1 virtual-link 18.1.4.1 missing on r4
Fix: `vtysh -c "router ospf" -c "area 1 virtual-link 18.1.4.1"`
  on r4

[FIX]
`vtysh -c "router ospf" -c "area 1 virtual-link 18.1.4.1"`
  on r4

[DIAGNOSTIC-PROCESS]
1. Scope the missing prefixes: confirm 18.x loopbacks and 80.0.0.0/8 are
   absent from R1's RIB while Area 0 and Area 1 routes remain; this
   narrows the fault to Area 2 connectivity.
2. Check OSPF area/LSA state: `show ip ospf` on R4 -- verify Area 2 LSAs
   are no longer being received; check that the virtual interface (vlink0)
   is absent from `show ip ospf interface`.
3. Identify the missing virtual-link: inspect R4's OSPF running config to
   confirm `area 1 virtual-link 18.1.4.1` is missing; this was the
   virtual-link tunneled through Area 1 to reach R8 (the Area 2 ABR).
4. Explain the transit-area mechanism: Area 2 has no direct connection to
   Area 0; it relies on the virtual-link through Area 1 (via R4<->R8) to
   inject its LSAs into the backbone; without it R8 cannot reach Area 0.
5. Localize to the lowest-layer root cause: `area 1 virtual-link 18.1.4.1`
   is missing on R4; rule out a link failure between R4 and R8 or an R8
   configuration change.
