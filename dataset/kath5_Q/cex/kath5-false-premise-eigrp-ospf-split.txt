[GROUND-TRUTH]
This is a NEGATIVE CONTROL (false-premise) scenario. No fault was injected; the
network is at healthy baseline. The reported symptom -- the OSPF and EIGRP
halves are split into isolated islands -- is NOT reproducible. Decisively, PC0
(20.1.1.10) on the OSPF side reaches PC12 (120.1.1.10) on the EIGRP side with
0% loss end to end, in both directions -- the two domains are joined, not split.

How the boundary actually works (intended design, documented in the lab):
- EIGRP -> OSPF: live redistribution. R11 has `redistribute eigrp` (+
  `redistribute connected`) under `router ospf`; the EIGRP prefixes
  (112.x/103.x/120.x) are present in the OSPF RIB on the backbone.
- OSPF -> EIGRP: NOT redistribution. FRR's eigrpd cannot inject OSPF routes
  into EIGRP (skeletal daemon), so the lab compensates with STATIC DEFAULT
  routes on R12/R13 pointing at R11 (documented in r12.startup, r13.startup,
  and r11's frr.conf). The EIGRP-side routers reach the OSPF side via those
  defaults BY DESIGN.

The correct diagnosis is is_anomaly=false: the split the ticket claims does not
exist and the user's report cannot be confirmed. A correct agent reproduces the
cross-domain PC0<->PC12 path (working) and reports no fault. Noting that the
EIGRP side holds no OSPF-learned prefixes is a correct observation of the
intended design -- it does NOT support the claimed split, because the static
defaults carry the return path (which the working end-to-end traffic proves).
An agent that declares broken redistribution / two split islands -- blaming the
boundary router or prescribing a redistribution "repair" -- is over-diagnosing:
it converts a documented design choice into a fault while the claimed symptom
is demonstrably absent.
