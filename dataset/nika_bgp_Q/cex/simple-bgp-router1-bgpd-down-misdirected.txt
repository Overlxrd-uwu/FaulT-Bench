==================================================
Scenario: simple-bgp-router1-bgpd-down-misdirected
Topology: simple_bgp
Fault family: routing daemon down (misdirected ticket)
==================================================

[PROMPT-TO-AGENT]
Helpdesk ticket #9101: A user on pc1 (195.11.14.2) reports they have completely
lost connectivity to the partner network -- they cannot reach pc2 (200.1.1.2) in
the peer AS since this morning. The team is convinced the peer's router, router2,
has fallen over and want router2 restarted. Please find and confirm the real
fault and tell us which device to restart.

[INJECTION]
device: router1
action: kill the BGP routing daemon (and its watchdog) on router1
cmd: pkill -9 watchfrr; pkill -9 bgpd

[POST-INJECT-CHECK]
router1   pgrep bgpd               => expect empty
pc1       ping -W2 -c2 200.1.1.2   => expect loss>0

[GROUND-TRUTH]
The real fault is on router1: its BGP routing daemon (bgpd) has been killed, so
the eBGP session between router1 and router2 drops and router1 loses/withdraws
the inter-AS routes. With no route to the peer AS, pc1 (195.11.14.2) cannot reach
pc2 (200.1.1.2). The ticket MISDIRECTS to router2 (the peer's router), but the
fault is on the LOCAL side: router1 is the eBGP speaker whose daemon is dead.
router2 is healthy -- its bgpd is still running and its inter-AS interface is up;
it simply has no session because router1 stopped speaking BGP.

The correct diagnosis identifies router1 (bgpd down) as the faulty device and
explicitly EXONERATES router2: router2's BGP daemon and interfaces are fine, so
the user's "router2 has fallen over" hypothesis is wrong. An agent that restarts
/ blames router2 has followed the ticket's misdirection instead of the evidence.

Observable note (lab-verified 2026-07-03): bgpd is killed together with watchfrr (its watchdog), so nothing respawns it. vtysh remains responsive via zebra, `show running-config` simply contains NO `router bgp` section, and `show daemons` no longer lists bgpd. An agent may therefore correctly describe this fault as "the bgp configuration is missing/empty on router1" -- that is the same fault observed at the config plane, and must NOT be treated as a wrong root cause.

[FIX]
Restart FRR/bgpd on router1 (`service frr restart`). Once bgpd is running again,
the eBGP session to router2 re-establishes, router1 re-learns and re-advertises
the inter-AS prefixes, and pc1 reaches pc2 normally. router2 needs no change.

[DIAGNOSTIC-PROCESS]
1. Reproduce on the real path: from pc1, ping pc2 (200.1.1.2) -- it fails (no
   route to the peer AS).
2. Check the eBGP session from both ends: on router1 the session to router2 is
   down and bgpd is not running (pgrep bgpd is empty); router2's bgpd is up but
   reports the peer (router1) as down.
3. Localize to the LOCAL speaker: the dead daemon on router1 fully explains the
   withdrawn inter-AS routes and the loss of reachability.
4. Localize to router1 (bgpd down), not router2: exonerate router2, whose daemon
   and inter-AS interface are healthy.

[SCORING-AXES]
assert-identifies: router1
assert-not-identifies: router2
must-identify-bgpd-down: identifies bgpd on router1 is down (local eBGP speaker), not a fault on router2
