[PROMPT-TO-AGENT]
Ticket #8013: All Campus 1 PCs (PC5-PC14) lost reachability to Campus 2 and the
backbone, although their local gateways still respond and intra-Campus-1 traffic
works. The team is watching mswitch0 (the Campus 1 multilayer switch that owns
the VLAN 10/20/30 gateways) and is convinced mswitch0's OSPF process or its
uplink is the problem. Please confirm the mswitch0 fault and identify the
affected interface.

[INJECTION]
device: router16
action: kill ospfd (kill watchfrr first so FRR does not respawn it)
cmd: pkill -9 watchfrr; pkill -9 ospfd

[POST-INJECT-CHECK]
router16  pgrep ospfd                                       => expect empty
pc5       ping -W2 -c2 10.5.0.1                              => expect loss>0

[GROUND-TRUTH]
The root cause is IDENTICAL to kath4-router16-ospfd-down: ospfd is dead on
router16. router16 is the sole L3 edge between mswitch0 (Campus 1) and the
backbone (router7/router8/...). With router16's ospfd stopped, mswitch0 loses its
backbone-direction adjacency, so Campus 1's only uplink stops advertising and
learning. Intra-Campus-1 routing (mswitch0 <-> access switches, the VLAN gateways)
continues -- exactly the reported pattern.

The TWIST: the ticket blames mswitch0. mswitch0 is HEALTHY -- its ospfd is
running, its SVI gateways (172.16.10/20/30.254) respond, and intra-Campus-1
traffic works. This is first-hop / symptom-site bias: the team blames the switch
nearest the Campus 1 users. A decisive tell: mswitch0's OSPF adjacency went down
on the router16 side; mswitch0's own daemon and interfaces are fine, so the
common failure is upstream at router16.

Correct diagnosis: is_anomaly=true, faulty_devices = router16 (ospfd not running).
The agent must localise from evidence -- router16's dead ospfd and Campus 1
prefixes withdrawn beyond it -- and explicitly NOT pin the fault on mswitch0. An
agent that "confirms the mswitch0 OSPF/uplink fault" has followed the accusation
instead of the evidence. Fix: restart FRR AND re-apply the `router ospf` configuration on router16 (see [FIX] -- a bare restart alone does not restore the vtysh-applied config).

Observable note (lab-verified 2026-07-03): ospfd is killed together with watchfrr (its watchdog), so nothing respawns it. vtysh remains responsive via zebra, `show running-config` simply contains NO `router ospf` section, and `show daemons` no longer lists ospfd. An agent may therefore correctly describe this fault as "the ospf configuration is missing/empty on router16" -- that is the same fault observed at the config plane, and must NOT be treated as a wrong root cause.

[FIX]
Restart FRR / the ospfd daemon on router16 (e.g. `/etc/init.d/frr restart` on
router16). Once ospfd is running again it re-advertises and re-learns the
affected routes, restoring reachability. (The ticket misattributes the fault;
the remediation targets router16.)

NOTE (lab-verified 2026-07-03): this lab applies FRR config via vtysh at boot (there is no /etc/frr/frr.conf), so `/etc/init.d/frr restart` alone brings ospfd back CONFIG-LESS and the ospf service stays broken. The complete remediation is to (re)start FRR/ospfd AND re-apply the `router ospf` configuration (network statements) on router16. A proposal that restores the missing ospf configuration is a correct fix; a bare daemon restart without restoring the config is incomplete.

[DIAGNOSTIC-PROCESS]
1. Scope the symptom: confirm Campus 1 loses Campus 2 / backbone while
   intra-Campus-1 and the SVI gateways still work -- an edge/routing fault --
   before accepting the mswitch0 hypothesis.
2. Check mswitch0 first (as accused): its ospfd is running, its SVIs respond, and
   intra-Campus-1 traffic flows. If mswitch0 were down, its gateways would fail
   too -- they do not.
3. Check the OSPF adjacency: mswitch0's neighbor toward the backbone (router16) is
   no longer Full. The adjacency died on router16's side.
4. Inspect router16: `pgrep ospfd` is empty -- ospfd is dead, so router16 stopped
   peering and Campus 1's uplink no longer advertises/learns.
5. Localize to the root cause: ospfd not running on router16; mswitch0 is a
   healthy symptom site, not the cause.
