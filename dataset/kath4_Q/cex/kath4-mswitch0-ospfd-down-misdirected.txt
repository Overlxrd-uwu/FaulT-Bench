[PROMPT-TO-AGENT]
Ticket #8041: All Campus 1 PCs (PC5-PC14) lost reachability to Campus 2
(10.5.0.x, 10.6.0.x) and the backbone, although intra-Campus-1 traffic still
works and the local gateways respond. The on-call engineer noticed router11
stopped showing the Campus 1 prefixes and is convinced router11 lost its routes.
Please restart router11 to recover the Campus 1 routes and confirm the router11
fault.

[INJECTION]
device: mswitch0
action: kill ospfd
cmd: pkill -9 watchfrr; pkill -9 ospfd

[POST-INJECT-CHECK]
mswitch0  pgrep ospfd                                          => expect empty
pc5       ping -W2 -c2 10.5.0.1                                => expect loss>0

[GROUND-TRUTH]
The root cause is IDENTICAL to kath4-mswitch0-ospfd-down: ospfd is dead on
mswitch0. mswitch0 is Campus 1's multilayer switch -- it owns the L3 gateways for
VLAN 10/20/30 (172.16.10/20/30.254) and runs OSPF to advertise those subnets into
the backbone. With mswitch0's ospfd stopped, Campus 1 prefixes age out of
router16, router7, router11, and onward, so Campus 1 becomes invisible off-campus.
Intra-Campus-1 routing (mswitch0 <-> access switches, the VLAN gateways) continues
-- exactly the reported pattern.

The TWIST: the ticket blames router11. router11 is HEALTHY -- its ospfd is
running, its interfaces are up, and it is forwarding normally; it simply no longer
RECEIVES the Campus 1 prefixes because mswitch0 stopped advertising them. router11
is a downstream observer of the withdrawal, not its source. Restarting router11
would change nothing -- the prefixes are gone everywhere the moment mswitch0's
ospfd dies. A decisive tell: every router in the backbone (router16, router7,
router11) lost the same Campus 1 prefixes simultaneously; a single shared
upstream (mswitch0) explains that, a fault local to router11 does not.

Correct diagnosis: is_anomaly=true, faulty_devices = mswitch0 (ospfd not running).
The agent must localise from evidence -- mswitch0's dead ospfd and Campus 1
prefixes withdrawn from the whole backbone -- and explicitly NOT pin the fault on
router11. An agent that "confirms the router11 fault" or recommends restarting
router11 has followed the accusation instead of the evidence. Fix: restart FRR AND re-apply the `router ospf` configuration on mswitch0 (see [FIX] -- a bare restart alone does not restore the vtysh-applied config).

Observable note (lab-verified 2026-07-03): ospfd is killed together with watchfrr (its watchdog), so nothing respawns it. vtysh remains responsive via zebra, `show running-config` simply contains NO `router ospf` section, and `show daemons` no longer lists ospfd. An agent may therefore correctly describe this fault as "the ospf configuration is missing/empty on mswitch0" -- that is the same fault observed at the config plane, and must NOT be treated as a wrong root cause.

[FIX]
`/etc/init.d/frr restart` on mswitch0

NOTE (lab-verified 2026-07-03): this lab applies FRR config via vtysh at boot (there is no /etc/frr/frr.conf), so `/etc/init.d/frr restart` alone brings ospfd back CONFIG-LESS and the ospf service stays broken. The complete remediation is to (re)start FRR/ospfd AND re-apply the `router ospf` configuration (network statements) on mswitch0. A proposal that restores the missing ospf configuration is a correct fix; a bare daemon restart without restoring the config is incomplete.

[DIAGNOSTIC-PROCESS]
1. Scope the symptom: confirm Campus 1 lost Campus 2 / backbone reachability while
   intra-Campus-1 and the VLAN gateways still work -- an advertiser/edge routing
   fault -- before accepting the router11 hypothesis.
2. Check router11 first (as accused): its ospfd is running and its interfaces are
   up; it forwards fine and simply no longer holds the Campus 1 prefixes. Nothing
   local to router11 failed, so restarting it would not restore the routes.
3. Observe the blast radius: router16, router7, AND router11 all lost the same
   Campus 1 prefixes at once. A single shared upstream advertiser (mswitch0)
   explains a simultaneous, network-wide withdrawal; a router11-local fault does
   not.
4. Check the OSPF adjacency and the advertiser: on router16 the adjacency toward
   mswitch0 is no longer Full -- mswitch0 stopped sending hellos. On mswitch0,
   `pgrep ospfd` is empty: ospfd is dead, so Campus 1's subnets are no longer
   advertised.
5. Localize to the root cause: ospfd not running on mswitch0; router11 is a
   healthy downstream observer of the withdrawal, not the cause.
