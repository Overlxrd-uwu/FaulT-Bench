==================================================
Scenario: kath4-router8-ospf-area-mismatch
Topology: Kath4
Fault family: OSPF area mismatch
==================================================

[PROMPT-TO-AGENT]
After a configuration change on router8, router7 reports that its
OSPF adjacency with router8 dropped (neighbor went INIT/DOWN, then
gone) and some Campus 2 routes flapped. Campus-1-to-Campus-1
traffic still works and Campus 2 PCs reach their local SVI. Find
what broke the router7<->router8 adjacency.

[INJECTION]
device: router8
action: change OSPF area for the 172.25.1.0 network to a
  mismatched area (area 7 instead of area 0)
cmd: |
  vtysh -c "configure terminal" \
        -c "router ospf" \
        -c "no network 172.25.1.0/24 area 0" \
        -c "network 172.25.1.0/24 area 7" \
        -c "exit" -c "exit"
  vtysh -c "clear ip ospf process"

[POST-INJECT-CHECK]
router7  vtysh -c "show ip ospf neighbor" | grep -c 172.29.10.1  => expect 0

[GROUND-TRUTH]
Affected component: router8 OSPF area configuration on the
  172.25.1.0/24 link
Mechanism: OSPF requires both ends of an adjacency to agree
  on the area ID. router7's eth5 is 172.25.1.1 in area 0;
  router8's eth4 was 172.25.1.2 in area 0 and was just changed
  to area 7. The hellos now carry mismatched area IDs, so the
  router7<->router8 adjacency drops (Full -> Init -> gone).
  router8 remains in OSPF via its OTHER area-0 links, so traffic
  reroutes and Campus 1 stays mostly reachable -- the observable,
  diagnosable fault is the dropped adjacency on the 172.25.1.0/24
  link, not a full Campus-2 outage.
Localization: router8
Root cause: OSPF area mismatch on the router7<->router8 link
  (router8 in area 7, router7 in area 0)
Fix: on router8 — `vtysh -c "router ospf" -c "no network
  172.25.1.0/24 area 7" -c "network 172.25.1.0/24 area 0"`

[FIX]
on router8 — `vtysh -c "router ospf" -c "no network
  172.25.1.0/24 area 7" -c "network 172.25.1.0/24 area 0"`

[DIAGNOSTIC-PROCESS]
1. Scope the symptom: confirm Campus 1 loses routes to Campus 2
   (172.29.10.0/24 and beyond) while Campus 1 internal traffic and
   Campus 2 intra-campus traffic still work; router7 shows the
   router8 adjacency in INIT or absent.
2. Check OSPF neighbor state: on router7, run `show ip ospf neighbor`
   — confirm the adjacency to 172.25.1.2 (router8) is stuck in INIT
   or is missing, not Full.
3. Compare area IDs on both ends of the link: check router7 eth5 (area
   0) against router8 eth4 — router8 now has 172.25.1.0/24 assigned
   to area 7, creating a mismatch that prevents hello acceptance.
4. Confirm the consequence: run `show ip route ospf` on router7 —
   172.29.10.0/24 and the Campus 2 prefixes are absent because
   router8 can no longer flood LSAs into area 0.
5. Localize to the lowest-layer root cause: router8's OSPF area
   assignment for 172.25.1.0/24 is wrong (area 7 vs area 0),
   ruling out a physical link failure or hello-timer mismatch.
